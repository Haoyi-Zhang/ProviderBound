# Reviewer challenge matrix

This matrix separates a resolved validity question from a limitation that cannot be removed without changing the research question. It is a navigation aid; the paper and retained machine-readable results are authoritative.

| Likely challenge | Resolution in the final package | Reproducible evidence | Residual boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Provider” is being presented as real ownership. | Owner is formally a caller-supplied label projection. The paper proves label coarsening, evaluates module, upstream-project, and third-party-role granularities, and reports the negative collapse of module ambiguity at coarser granularities. | Owner-granularity result records; exhaustive normalized label partitions; claim–evidence index. | No authorship, legal ownership, organizational control, or malicious provenance is inferred. |
| The Android motivation exceeds the evaluated semantics. | The title and abstract name first-winner archive merging. A scope contract excludes Android resources/manifests, variants, D8/R8, relocation, native code, reflection, and dynamic loading. | Formal input language; fail-closed parser tests; scope guardrails in the paper. | Findings apply only after a build step has been soundly lowered to the stated archive language. |
| Two-provider examples hide global-order bugs. | Four retained public multi-provider groups are externally built in all 42 permutations, yielding 14 observable output classes. Production, independent adapter, certificate checker, and literal permutation oracle agree. | Multi-provider inputs, per-order archives, raw summaries, and reproduction stage. | Four groups establish conformance for those cases, not ecosystem frequency. |
| The public corpus was cherry-picked. | The artifact preserves the complete 107 non-symlink JAR frame, 37 upstream-project labels, all 5,671 unordered pairs, and the deterministic collision filter that yields 43 providers and 40 colliding pairs. | Full archive frame, hashes, copyright records, and collision recomputation. | This is a census of one frozen environment, not a random or representative ecosystem sample. |
| The exact oracle does not cover label projections. | The finite oracle enumerates normalized owner partitions for one to three providers, totaling 676,354 labeled instances. | Exhaustive-oracle result and source. | Exhaustive enumeration is finite-model validation, not a proof for unbounded inputs; the paper supplies separate mathematical arguments. |
| The baselines are unfair or absent. | Three same-input information baselines are defined: output content only, independent local candidates, and complete known order. Their information advantages and deficits are explicit. | Equal-byte reversals, the mixed globally coupled case, and the full-order oracle. | Android library detectors and SBOM tools solve different input/output contracts and are not ranked as competitors. |

Certificates for possible labels and counterfactual blocking evidence are checked separately. | Negative tests, finite oracle, and checker rejection records. | Inconsistency diagnoses the supplied evidence; it does not identify which external input is wrong. |

## Decision rule

A challenge is marked resolved only when the released package contains (1) a scoped statement, (2) executable or mathematical support, (3) a negative or adversarial check where applicable, and (4) a residual-boundary statement. “No residual boundary” is not a credible standard for empirical software-engineering research.
